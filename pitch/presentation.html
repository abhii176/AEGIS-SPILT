The market

Two counts. They measure different things.

11.9 million

BLS, July 2023. Independent contractors whose contracting was their sole or main job. 7.4% of employment.

64 million

Upwork / Edelman, 2023. Americans who did any freelance work that year. 38% of the workforce in that survey. About $1.27 trillion in earnings. Not a GDP line.

Edelman surveyed 3,000 working adults, 24 Oct–9 Nov 2023. Margin of error ±1.7%.

their earnings in 2024. Upwork Future Workforce Index.

$85,000

The failure

Late pay is the ordinary case.

85%

of freelancers are paid late at least some of the time.

21%

are paid late, or not at all, more than half the time.

Remote, Contractor Management Report 2025, as published on Remote’s blog. Aegis-Split’s answer is a PayPal capture before the merge, not a reminder email.

Same module, three rails

What the freelancer keeps on $4,800.

$4,800

Aegis-Split. Platform fee 0%. The full milestone is the payout.

$4,080–$4,800

Upwork freelancer fee, 0–15% on a new contract, locked when it starts. A 10% contract nets $4,320. Pre-May 2025 contracts stay at 10%.

$3,840

Fiverr. The seller receives 80% of a completed order.

$4,800 is one module, not a surveyed average. The live judge demo is $250 so the click-through stays small. The fee rules are the published ones.

The hold

$4,800 in. $480 beside it.

$4,800.00

Client vault. 480,000 cents. PayPal Orders capture. This is the money the audit can release.

$480.00

Freelancer deposit. Default is floor of 10% of the milestone. A second PayPal order. Held until pay, exit, or a counselor decision.

Integer cents. Percentages use floor, so a split never invents a cent. 480,000 × 10 / 100 is exact.

If the merge passes

The freelancer is paid in full.

Payout

$4,800.00 through PayPal Payouts. Platform fee $0.00.

Deposit

$480.00 returned to the freelancer.

Trigger

A merged pull request, checked against criteria locked at create. GPT-4o mini when a key is set. Otherwise a keyword check. The public demo uses the keyword check.

If someone leaves

The contract already prices the exit.

Both agree

Vault $4,800 back to the client. Deposit $480 back to the freelancer.

Client cancels

Default 50% penalty. $2,400 to the freelancer, $2,400 refunded, deposit returned.

Freelancer walks

Vault $4,800 back to the client. Deposit $480 forfeited to the client.

If they disagree

A counselor moves the same cents.

$4,800

Release. Remaining vault to the freelancer.

$4,800

Refund. Remaining vault back to the client.

$2,400

Split, default 50%. Freelancer $2,400. Client refund $2,400. Floor keeps the extra cent with the client.

Deposit returns to the freelancer unless “Freelancer at fault” is checked, which forfeits the $480 to the client. Payout and exit wait until the decision.

In the product today

What the demo actually runs.

Running

PayPal vault, payout, and refund. 0% fee. Deposit, exit, and dispute math. On-platform chat. KYC sandbox plus signed consent. The auditor is GPT-4o mini when a key is set, otherwise a keyword check. The public demo uses the keyword check.

Not in this build

A real login. The demo sends X-User-Id. Live Agora calls, transcription, and stored recordings. The counselor card is a sample preview. Off-platform chat detection is not built.

The pitch film says GPT-4o and integrated Agora. This deck stays with the code.
